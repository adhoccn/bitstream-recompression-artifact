(A) Is a weighted log-sum still a likelihood-ratio test?
    statistic                         AUC
    true log-LR (equal weights)    0.8366
    weights [1, 1, 1]              0.8366   cos^2=1.0000
    weights [3, 1, 1]              0.8031   cos^2=0.7576
    weights [1, 3, 1]              0.8031   cos^2=0.7576
    weights [5, 1, 1]              0.7769   cos^2=0.6049
    weights [10, 1, 1]             0.7491   cos^2=0.4706
    -> equal weights reproduce the LRT; unequal weights do not,
       and the deficit grows as the weight vector tilts away
       from the all-ones direction. 'UMP' is therefore reserved
       for the unweighted (correctly-scaled) statistic.

(C) Cascade (OR) versus log-sum fusion, K = 4 stages
  -- dense alternative: every stage carries equal signal
      dense  delta = 0.50 on all stages
      rule                FPR      power
      OR/cascade       0.1859     0.4180
      log-sum          0.0500     0.2592
      max              0.0500     0.1545
      disagreement (H1): OR vs log-sum = 0.2514
  -- sparse alternative: exactly ONE stage carries signal
      sparse delta = 1.20 on one stage only
      rule                FPR      power
      OR/cascade       0.1852     0.4226
      log-sum          0.0500     0.1482
      max              0.0500     0.1824
      disagreement (H1): OR vs log-sum = 0.3153

    -> dense:  log-sum 0.2592 >= OR 0.4180  (log-sum wins, as Theorem 5 predicts)
    -> sparse: max/OR 0.1824 >= log-sum 0.1482  (the ORDER flips)
       i.e. under a sparse alternative the generalized LRT is
       max_k Lambda_k, and the cascade is the correct rule.
       The cascade is therefore an approximation to the LRT in
       the dense regime and the exact GLR rule in the sparse one.
